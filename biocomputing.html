Research Area · Living Computation

Biocomputing

My biocomputing research investigates how biological processes can be used as the computational medium itself, with living cells and biological networks performing information transformation rather than merely supplying data to conventional processors.

Living cellsWet computingNon-silicon computation
Research perspective

What I investigate

Biocomputing replaces or complements silicon-based computation with biological mechanisms. My work focuses especially on bacterial gene regulation, where molecular signaling, regulatory feedback, and cellular plasticity can be interpreted and engineered as computational operations. This creates opportunities for intelligent processing directly inside biological environments where conventional electronics are difficult to deploy.

Molecular inputs

Encoding information through concentrations, signaling molecules, and time-varying molecular stimuli.

Cellular processing

Using intracellular regulatory interactions and feedback as nonlinear signal-processing mechanisms.

Distributed computation

Leveraging populations of cells and interacting biological components for parallel and distributed information processing.

Reconfigurable function

Designing biological architectures whose computational role can be changed through parameters, signals, or network configuration.

Scientific approach

From biological dynamics to computational function

Research philosophy

The distinctive opportunity in biocomputing is not to make biology behave exactly like a digital computer, but to exploit what living systems already do well: sensing, adaptation, nonlinear response, distributed coordination, and operation in biochemical environments.

Information encoding

Represent computational inputs as molecular or environmental perturbations.

Biological transformation

Allow living regulatory processes to transform those inputs through their natural dynamics.

State measurement

Measure gene expression, molecular state, or population response.

Computational decoding

Map biological state to a useful output, class, control action, or mathematical result.

Application integration

Embed the biological processor into sensing, communication, or adaptive decision systems.
