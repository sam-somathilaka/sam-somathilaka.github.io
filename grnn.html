Research Area · Bacterial Computing

Gene Regulatory Neural Networks

I study bacterial gene-regulatory networks as neural-like computational systems, where molecular interactions provide nonlinear transformations, state dependence, and reconfigurable information processing inside living cells.

GRNNBacterial biocomputingMolecular machine learning
Research perspective

What I investigate

A Gene Regulatory Neural Network (GRNN) interprets regulatory genes and their interactions through a computational lens: regulatory inputs act as signals, gene interactions implement nonlinear transformations, and gene-expression states provide outputs that can be mapped to computational tasks. My research develops both the conceptual framework and practical architectures for using these networks as living, reconfigurable computing elements.

Network-to-computation mapping

Translating gene-regulatory topology, activation/repression logic, and molecular kinetics into computational primitives that can be analyzed and composed.

Mathematical solvers

Designing GRNN architectures that approximate mathematical operations and nonlinear mappings as a reusable biological computing library.

Reconfigurability

Changing network parameters, molecular inputs, or pathway selection to repurpose the same biological machinery for different computational goals.

Inverse signal programming

Using language-model-guided reasoning to infer molecular signal programs that drive a GRNN toward desired output behaviors.

Scientific approach

From biological dynamics to computational function

Research philosophy

GRNNs provide a route to computation that is molecular, massively parallel, and naturally embedded in living cells—while remaining interpretable through the regulatory mechanisms that generate the computation.

Model

Represent transcriptional regulation and molecular kinetics as a dynamical computational network.

Encode inputs

Map molecular concentrations or signaling patterns to regulatory inputs.

Transform

Exploit activation, repression, feedback, and cross-regulation as nonlinear computation.

Decode outputs

Read target gene states or downstream molecular responses as task outputs.

Reconfigure

Modify the network or signal program to solve a new computational objective.
