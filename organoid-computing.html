Research Area · Living Neural Systems

Organoid Computing

I investigate living neuronal cultures as physical reservoirs for computation, using their intrinsic nonlinear dynamics, temporal memory, population states, and adaptive responses to transform time-varying inputs.

Neuronal reservoir computingMEA systemsClosed-loop control
Research perspective

What I investigate

My work in organoid and neuronal computing focuses on reservoir computing with living neural networks cultured on multi-electrode arrays. Rather than engineering a fixed artificial network architecture, I characterize and exploit the culture’s naturally evolving dynamics. Electrical stimulation provides inputs, distributed spiking activity forms the reservoir state, and lightweight readout layers translate those states into computational outputs.

Intrinsic dynamics

Quantifying spontaneous activity, nonlinear responses, fading memory, separability, synchrony, and state transitions in living neuronal networks.

MEA stimulation and recording

Delivering structured electrical inputs through selected electrodes and measuring distributed evoked population responses.

Reservoir computation

Using the culture as a high-dimensional nonlinear transformation whose states are decoded with compact readout models.

Closed-loop applications

Connecting neuronal-reservoir predictions to real-time control tasks, including dynamical-system prediction and game control.

Scientific approach

From biological dynamics to computational function

Research philosophy

The key idea is to compute with the neuronal network that is already there: characterize its natural dynamics, stimulate it in a controlled way, and use the resulting population activity as a computational state space.

Characterize

Measure spontaneous firing, population synchrony, active-electrode fraction, drift, and state-space structure.

Screen inputs

Identify stimulation electrodes and parameters that generate separable, reliable responses.

Stimulate

Encode task inputs as electrical stimulation patterns.

Record reservoir state

Extract distributed spike features across the MEA over task-relevant time windows.

Train readout

Fit lightweight readout layers for classification, prediction, or control while leaving the living reservoir itself largely unengineered.

Current & recent work

Research directions

These themes connect mechanistic biological modeling with experimentally grounded computation and lightweight machine-learning readouts.

01

Nonlinearity and memory

Evaluating superposition failure, XOR computation, fading memory, and separability as core reservoir-computing properties.

02

Chaotic-system prediction

Using neuronal reservoir states to predict nonlinear dynamical systems such as double-scroll and Lorenz trajectories.

03

State-aware computation

Studying how synchronized, near-chaotic, and sparse activity regimes alter computational performance and input separability.

04

Closed-loop control

Testing whether biological-reservoir predictions are accurate and stable enough for online control tasks.
